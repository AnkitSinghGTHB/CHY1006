# Water Footprint and Virtual Water

## 10 Points

1. **Water Footprint (Definition)** — Total freshwater used (directly + indirectly) to produce goods/services. Coined by **Arjen Hoekstra (2002)**. Global average: ~3,800 L/person/day (mostly indirect via food & products).

2. **Green Water Footprint** — Rainwater consumed by plants via evapotranspiration. Example: rainfed wheat using monsoon rain. Most sustainable type — doesn't deplete rivers/groundwater.

3. **Blue Water Footprint** — Surface/groundwater withdrawn and NOT returned. Example: irrigated rice paddies, industrial cooling. High blue footprint = water stress risk.

4. **Grey Water Footprint** — Freshwater needed to **dilute pollutants** to acceptable standards. Example: water to dilute fertiliser runoff. High grey footprint = heavy pollution.

5. **Virtual Water (Definition)** — Water "embedded" in a product. Coined by **J.A. Allan (1993)**. 1 kg beef = ~15,400 L, 1 kg rice = ~2,500 L, 1 pair jeans = ~10,000 L, 1 cup coffee = ~140 L.

6. **Virtual Water Trade** — Importing goods = importing virtual water. Water-scarce countries import water-intensive products instead of growing them. Global virtual water trade: ~2,320 B m³/year. India is a net EXPORTER (rice, cotton).

7. **National Differences** — USA ~2,842 m³/person/year (high meat diet), India ~1,089 m³ (large agriculture), UK ~1,695 m³ (imports virtual water). Meat-heavy diets = 5–10× larger footprint.

8. **Green Footprint → Conservation** — Promotes rainfed agriculture, rainwater harvesting, and locally suited crops. Reduces pressure on rivers and groundwater.

9. **Blue + Grey → Overuse Signals** — High blue = aquifer depletion (Punjab, Rajasthan). High grey = fertiliser/industrial pollution. Solutions: drip irrigation (saves 30–60%), organic farming, zero liquid discharge.

10. **Global Water Security** — 2B people in water-stressed areas. Demand may exceed supply by 40% by 2030. Water footprint tools help align crop choices with water availability and support SDG 6 (Clean Water).
