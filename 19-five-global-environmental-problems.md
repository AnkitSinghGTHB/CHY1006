# Five Global Environmental Problems Linked to Global Temperature Rise

## 10 Points (5 Problems × 2 Points Each)

### 1. Melting of Polar Ice Caps and Glaciers
- Arctic sea ice is shrinking by ~13% per decade. Antarctic & Greenland ice sheets losing ~430 billion tonnes of ice/year.
- Himalayan glaciers (water source for 1.5B people) are retreating. Threatens water supply for rivers like Ganga, Brahmaputra, Indus.

### 2. Sea Level Rise and Coastal Flooding
- Global sea level has risen ~20 cm since 1900 and is accelerating (~3.6 mm/year now). Caused by thermal expansion + ice melt.
- Threatens low-lying nations (Maldives, Bangladesh, Pacific islands). ~680 million people live in low coastal zones. Could displace 200M+ by 2100.

### 3. Extreme Weather Events
- Warming fuels more intense cyclones, hurricanes, heatwaves, droughts, and floods. Warm oceans = stronger storms.
- India: increasing frequency of severe cyclones (Amphan, Tauktae), deadly heatwaves (50°C+ in Rajasthan), and erratic monsoons affecting agriculture.

### 4. Ocean Acidification and Coral Bleaching
- Oceans absorb ~25% of CO₂ → forms carbonic acid → pH dropped 0.1 (30% more acidic). Harder for corals/shellfish to build CaCO₃ skeletons.
- Coral bleaching: warm water expels symbiotic algae → corals turn white → die. 50%+ of Great Barrier Reef degraded. Coral reefs support 25% of marine species.

### 5. Biodiversity Loss and Ecosystem Shifts
- Species can't adapt fast enough. Climate zones shifting 60 km/decade toward poles. Mountain-top species have nowhere to go → extinction.
- 1.5°C warming = 20–30% species at increased risk of extinction. Polar bears, penguins, pikas, and coral-dependent fish among the most vulnerable.
