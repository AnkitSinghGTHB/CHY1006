# Biodiversity Conservation Strategies & Human Impact on Extinction

## 10 Points

1. **Protected Areas (In-Situ)** — National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves. India has 100+ national parks (Jim Corbett, Kaziranga) protecting tigers & rhinos.

2. **Ex-Situ Conservation** — Zoos, seed banks, gene banks. Svalbard Seed Vault stores 1.1M+ seed samples. California Condor bred back from 27 to 500+.

3. **Hotspot Protection** — 36 biodiversity hotspots cover 2.5% of land but hold 50% of plant species. India has 4 hotspots (Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma, Sundaland).

4. **Legal Frameworks** — CBD (1992), Wildlife Protection Act (1972), Biological Diversity Act (2002). Laws criminalise poaching and regulate resource use.

5. **Community Conservation** — Joint Forest Management (JFM) empowers local villagers. Kenya's community conservancies stabilised elephant populations.

6. **Habitat Restoration** — Wildlife corridors connect fragmented habitats. Yellowstone-to-Yukon (Y2Y) corridor spans 3,200 km for bears and wolves.

7. **Sustainable Resource Use** — Fishing quotas, organic farming, sacred groves. MSC certifies sustainable seafood to prevent overexploitation.

8. **Human Impact** — Extinction rate is 100–1,000× the natural rate. Major causes: deforestation (10M hectares/year), fossil fuels, invasive species via global trade.

9. **Education & Citizen Science** — Programs like eBird, iNaturalist, Project Tiger. India's tiger count rose from ~1,400 (2006) to 3,600+ (2023) with public support.

10. **Biotechnology** — Cryopreservation, DNA barcoding, Frozen Zoo (10,000+ individuals stored). These tools can help revive declining or recently extinct species.
