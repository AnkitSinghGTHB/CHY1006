# Ecosystem — Definition and Biotic Components

## 10 Points

1. **Definition** — Functional unit of nature where living organisms interact with each other and their physical environment, exchanging energy and materials. Term coined by **Arthur Tansley (1935)**.

2. **Types** — Terrestrial (forest, grassland, desert, tundra), Freshwater (ponds, lakes, rivers, wetlands), Marine (oceans, coral reefs, estuaries, mangroves), Artificial (croplands, aquaculture).

3. **Biotic Components** — Classified by nutritional role into three groups: **Producers** (autotrophs), **Consumers** (heterotrophs), and **Decomposers** (saprotrophs).

4. **Producers (Autotrophs)** — Make their own food. Plants & algae use photosynthesis (CO₂ + H₂O → glucose). Phytoplankton produce ~50% of Earth's oxygen. Form base of every food chain.

5. **Primary Consumers (Herbivores)** — Eat producers directly. Examples: deer, grasshopper, zooplankton, caterpillar. Have flat grinding teeth and long digestive tracts. Only 10% energy transfers up.

6. **Secondary Consumers** — Eat herbivores. Examples: frog (eats insects), snake (eats rodents), small fish (eats zooplankton), fox (eats rabbits). Adapted for hunting (claws, speed, senses).

7. **Tertiary / Apex Predators** — Top of food chain, no natural predators. Examples: eagle, tiger, shark, lion. Few in number. Control ecosystem via top-down regulation (trophic cascades).

8. **Omnivores** — Eat both plants and animals. Examples: humans, bears, crows, pigs. Provide flexibility to food webs — can switch food sources when one is scarce.

9. **Decomposers & Detritivores** — Break down dead matter → recycle nutrients (C, N, P) back to soil. Bacteria + fungi = decomposers. Earthworms, dung beetles, vultures = detritivores. 1g soil has ~1B bacteria.

10. **Biotic Interactions** — Predation (lion–deer), Competition (trees for sunlight), Mutualism (bee–flower), Commensalism (orchid on tree), Parasitism (tapeworm in gut). These drive energy flow and population dynamics.
