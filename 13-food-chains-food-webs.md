# Food Chains, Food Webs, and Trophic Levels

## 10 Points

1. **Grazing Food Chain (GFC)** — Starts with plants → herbivores → carnivores. Example: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle.

2. **Detritus Food Chain (DFC)** — Starts with dead organic matter → detritivores → predators. Example: Dead leaves → Earthworm → Chicken → Fox. Handles ~80% of energy in forests.

3. **Food Web** — Interconnected network of multiple food chains. More realistic — a frog eats grasshoppers AND beetles; a hawk eats snakes AND rabbits. Shows multiple feeding relationships.

4. **Trophic Levels** — T1: Producers (grass), T2: Primary consumers/herbivores (deer), T3: Secondary consumers (frog), T4: Tertiary consumers (snake), T5: Apex predators (eagle). Decomposers operate at all levels.

5. **Producers (T1) — Significance** — Foundation of all food chains. Fix 120B tonnes carbon/year via photosynthesis. Generate all atmospheric oxygen. Phytoplankton = 50% of Earth's O₂.

6. **Herbivores (T2) — Significance** — Link producers to carnivores. Control plant populations. Selective grazing promotes plant diversity. Most species in ecosystems are at T1 or T2.

7. **Carnivores (T3, T4) — Significance** — Regulate herbivore populations, preventing overgrazing. Wolves removed from Yellowstone → elk overgrazing → adding wolves back restored the ecosystem (trophic cascade).

8. **Apex Predators (T5) — Significance** — Keystone species with disproportionate impact. Control mesopredators via top-down regulation. Sea otters eat urchins → protect kelp forests. Their loss triggers ecosystem collapse.

9. **10% Law (Lindeman, 1942)** — Only ~10% energy transfers to next level; 90% lost as heat. So: 10,000 kcal → 1,000 → 100 → 10. That's why food chains rarely exceed 4–5 levels and apex predators are rare.

10. **Ecological Pyramids** — Pyramid of Numbers: usually upright (can be inverted — 1 tree, many insects). Pyramid of Biomass: upright on land, inverted in ocean. Pyramid of **Energy: ALWAYS upright** — energy always decreases.
