# Primary and Secondary Air Pollutants — Definition, Examples, Health & Vegetation Effects

## 10 Points

1. **Primary Pollutants** — Emitted **directly** from sources in harmful form. Examples: CO (vehicles), SO₂ (coal plants), NOₓ (engines), PM₂.₅ (diesel), VOCs (paints), Lead (smelting), NH₃ (farming).

2. **Secondary Pollutants** — Formed in atmosphere when primary pollutants react. Examples: Ozone (O₃), PAN, H₂SO₄ (acid rain), photochemical smog. NOT emitted directly.

3. **CO — Silent Killer** — Binds haemoglobin 240× stronger than O₂ → reduces oxygen transport → headaches, dizziness, death at high levels.

4. **SO₂ & NO₂** — Irritate airways, worsen asthma and bronchitis. Long-term exposure increases respiratory infections and lung damage.

5. **PM₂.₅ — Deadliest** — Particles ≤2.5µm penetrate deep into lungs and bloodstream → causes heart attacks, strokes, lung cancer. Kills ~4.2 million/year (WHO).

6. **Lead (Pb)** — Neurotoxin. Even low exposure causes irreversible brain damage in children, reduces IQ. No safe level exists.

7. **Ground-level Ozone (O₃)** — Secondary pollutant. Inflames lungs, reduces lung function. Causes leaf stippling in plants and reduces crop yields by 5–15% globally.

8. **PAN (Peroxyacetyl Nitrate)** — Extremely toxic to plants. Damages mesophyll cells, causes silvering/bronzing of leaves. Also irritates human eyes.

9. **Acid Rain** — SO₂ + NOₓ → H₂SO₄ + HNO₃ in rain (pH < 5.6). Kills aquatic life, leaches soil nutrients, corrodes monuments (Taj Mahal).

10. **Synergistic Effects** — Combined pollutants are worse than individual. SO₂ + PM₂.₅ together cause more lung damage. NOₓ + VOCs + sunlight → photochemical smog.
