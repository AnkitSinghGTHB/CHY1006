# Phosphorus Cycle (with Diagram)

## Diagram

```
    PHOSPHATE ROCKS (Apatite minerals)
         │                │
    WEATHERING        MINING → Fertilisers
    (rain, wind)          │
         │                │
         ▼                ▼
    SOIL (PO₄³⁻ dissolved phosphate)
         │
    PLANT UPTAKE → DNA, RNA, ATP, bones
         │
    ANIMALS eat plants → P in bones, teeth
         │
    DEATH → DECOMPOSITION → PO₄³⁻ back to soil
         │
    RUNOFF → Rivers → OCEAN SEDIMENTS
         │
    (Millions of years) → SEDIMENTARY ROCK
         │
    TECTONIC UPLIFT → New rock exposed → cycle repeats
```

## 10 Points

1. **No Gas Phase** — Unlike C and N, phosphorus has NO atmospheric form. Cycles only through rock, soil, water, and organisms. Slowest biogeochemical cycle (10–100 million years).

2. **Source: Rocks** — Cycle starts with phosphate rocks (apatite). Weathering by rain/wind releases PO₄³⁻ into soil. Only natural input of new phosphorus.

3. **Plant Uptake** — Plants absorb PO₄³⁻ via roots (helped by mycorrhizal fungi). Used to build DNA, RNA, ATP (energy), and phospholipids (cell membranes).

4. **Food Chain** — Animals get P by eating plants. Critical for bones and teeth (as calcium phosphate — Ca₃(PO₄)₂), muscle contraction, and nerve signalling.

5. **Decomposition** — Dead organisms broken down by bacteria → PO₄³⁻ released back to soil. Guano (seabird droppings) is a rich phosphorus source.

6. **Ocean Sedimentation** — Runoff carries P to oceans → marine organisms use it → their remains settle as sediment → becomes rock after millions of years (tectonic uplift completes cycle).

7. **Human Disruption: Mining** — ~220M tonnes phosphate rock mined/year for fertilisers. Reserves may deplete in 50–100 years ("Peak Phosphorus").

8. **Eutrophication** — Excess P from runoff/sewage → algal blooms → O₂ depletion → dead zones → fish kills. Lake Erie is a well-known example.

9. **Limiting Nutrient** — P is the limiting nutrient in most freshwater ecosystems. Even small additions trigger massive algal growth. Key target for water conservation.

10. **Sustainability** — Solutions: precision fertiliser use, phosphorus recovery from sewage/manure, reducing runoff with buffer zones. Closing the loop is critical for food security.
