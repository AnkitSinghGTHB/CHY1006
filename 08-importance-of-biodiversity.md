# Importance of Biodiversity, Hotspots, and Endemic Species

## 10 Points

1. **Ecological Stability** — Diverse ecosystems recover better from disturbances. A 50-species forest survives disease better than a monoculture (insurance hypothesis).

2. **Ecosystem Services** — Worth $125–145 trillion/year. Includes pollination ($235B), water purification, carbon storage, flood control, soil formation.

3. **Medicinal Value** — 50%+ modern medicines come from nature. Aspirin (willow bark), penicillin (mould), taxol (yew tree), artemisinin (Artemisia plant).

4. **Food Security** — Wild crop relatives carry disease/drought-resistant genes. Only 30 crops provide 95% of food energy — genetic diversity is insurance.

5. **Economic Importance** — Ecotourism worth $600B+/year. Coral reefs generate ~$36B through tourism, fisheries, coastal protection. Millions depend on forests.

6. **Cultural Value** — Lotus in Hinduism, bald eagle (USA), cherry blossoms (Japan). Species shape art, identity, and spiritual practices across cultures.

7. **Biodiversity Hotspot (Definition)** — Region with ≥1,500 endemic plant species AND ≥70% habitat lost. 36 hotspots exist, covering 2.5% of land but holding 50% of plant species.

8. **Examples of Hotspots** — Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Sundaland, Mediterranean Basin, Tropical Andes (richest — 30,000 plant species).

9. **Endemic Species** — Found ONLY in one specific area, nowhere else. Lion-tailed macaque (Western Ghats), Nilgiri Tahr (~3,000 left). Irreplaceable if lost.

10. **Climate Regulation** — Forests absorb 2.6B tonnes CO₂/year. Mangroves sequester 3–5× more carbon than land forests. Peatlands (3% of land) store 2× all forest carbon.
