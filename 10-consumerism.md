# Consumerism — Concept, Impact, Advantages, and Disadvantages

## 10 Points

1. **Definition** — Ideology that encourages ever-increasing acquisition of goods. Equates well-being with material possessions. Consumer spending = 60–70% of GDP in developed countries.

2. **Economic Advantage** — Drives growth, innovation, and employment. Global consumer market ~$60 trillion/year. Competition leads to better products (smartphones, EVs, medical devices).

3. **Economic Disadvantage** — Creates boom-bust cycles (2008 crisis). Planned obsolescence wastes resources. Wealth inequality — rich nations benefit while exploiting cheap labour (e.g., Bangladesh garments).

4. **Social Advantage** — Improved living standards (clean water, healthcare, tech). More consumer choices at lower prices. Led to consumer protection laws (Consumer Protection Act, FDA).

5. **Social Disadvantage** — Materialism → anxiety, depression (hedonic treadmill). Wealthiest 10% cause ~50% of emissions. Erodes local cultures/crafts. Aggressive advertising targets children.

6. **Environmental: Resource Depletion** — 100B+ tonnes raw materials extracted/year. We consume ~3.5 Earths' worth of resources. Critical minerals (lithium, cobalt) face depletion.

7. **Environmental: Waste** — 2B tonnes municipal waste/year (projected 3.4B by 2050). 54M tonnes e-waste (only 17% recycled). 8M tonnes plastic enters oceans yearly. Fast fashion = 92M tonnes textile waste/year.

8. **Carbon Footprint** — Consumer activities = ~60% of global GHG emissions. Average American footprint (~16t CO₂) is 8× the 2t target for 1.5°C. Food, housing, transport are biggest contributors.

9. **Sustainable Alternatives** — Circular economy (reuse, repair, recycle), minimalism (buy less but better), sharing economy (ride-sharing, rentals), Right to Repair movement.

10. **Way Forward** — EPR policies, carbon taxes, plastic bans, ESG reporting for corporates, environmental education, eco-labels, supporting ethical brands, measuring personal footprint.
