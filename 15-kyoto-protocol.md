# Kyoto Protocol — Importance, Mechanisms, and Success/Failure

## 10 Points

1. **What & When** — International treaty under UNFCCC committing industrialised nations to binding GHG emission cuts. Adopted **11 Dec 1997** (Kyoto, Japan), entered force **16 Feb 2005**.

2. **Why It Was Formed** — IPCC showed human emissions cause warming. UNFCCC (1992) was voluntary & insufficient. CO₂ had risen from 280 → 360 ppm. Based on **CBDR** — developed nations should lead since they caused most historical emissions.

3. **Commitments** — 37 developed countries (Annex I) pledged average **5.2% reduction** below 1990 levels (2008–2012). Covered 6 gases: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, HFCs, PFCs, SF₆. Developing countries had NO binding targets.

4. **Mechanism 1: Emissions Trading** — Cap-and-trade system. Countries exceeding targets can sell surplus credits. Created carbon markets worth billions. Inspired the EU ETS.

5. **Mechanism 2: CDM (Clean Development Mechanism)** — Developed countries fund clean projects in developing countries, earn emission credits. 8,000+ projects registered. India & China were largest hosts. Promoted tech transfer.

6. **Mechanism 3: Joint Implementation (JI)** — Annex I country invests in emission-reduction projects in another Annex I country (e.g., Germany funds efficiency project in Poland). Less used than CDM.

7. **Achievements** — First legally binding climate treaty. Created carbon markets. Annex I countries (excluding US) reduced emissions by ~22.6% below 1990 levels by 2012, exceeding the 5.2% target.

8. **Failures** — US never ratified (world's top emitter then). China & India had no targets despite being major emitters. Canada withdrew in 2011. Soviet economic collapse inflated results ("hot air"). Global emissions still rose ~40%.

9. **Second Period (Doha, 2012)** — Extended to 2013–2020 with 18% target. But US, Russia, Japan, Canada didn't join. Covered only ~15% of global emissions. Limited practical impact.

10. **Legacy** — Paved the way for **Paris Agreement (2015)**, which learned from Kyoto's weaknesses: all countries now have targets (NDCs), not just developed ones. Kyoto's carbon market mechanisms refined into Paris Article 6.
